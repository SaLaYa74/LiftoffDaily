This view is 230 light-years across 🤯

This is N159, a star-forming region 161,000 light-years away in the Large Magellanic Cloud, a small galaxy orbiting the Milky Way. This one view spans about 230 light-years.
The orange glow comes from polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, carbon-based molecules; the pale blue is hydrogen gas ionized by massive young stars.
The dark red filaments are cold molecular hydrogen, the densest part of the cloud, where new stars are forming.
