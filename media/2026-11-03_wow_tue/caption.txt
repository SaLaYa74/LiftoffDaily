These aren't mountains 🤯

These "Cosmic Cliffs" are the edge of a gigantic cavity in the star-forming region NGC 3324, in the Carina Nebula, roughly 7,600 light-years away.
The "steam" rising off them is hot, ionized gas and dust streaming away, pushed by ultraviolet light from massive young stars.
Webb's infrared camera revealed hundreds of stars that were hidden behind the dust.
