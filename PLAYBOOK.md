## 2. What our other two pages taught us

After about 70 Reels, Paycheck Math and SharpCharts each reach **~100-140 people per Reel**. They have **zero shares and zero saves**, and **12 followers between them**. People watch 2-5 seconds.

That ~100 is Instagram's first test audience. A Reel only gets pushed further when that first group **shares it, rewatches it, or follows**. Animated charts over a synth beat don't make anyone think "I have to send this to someone."

**What this page does differently:**

| Old pages | Liftoff Daily |
|---|---|
| Charts on a flat background | Real NASA/ESA photos and footage with slow cinematic motion |
| Facts with no story | Every Reel has a hook, a payoff and a "wait, what?" ending |
| Same rotation every week | Built around events: "the meteor shower is TONIGHT" lands the day it happens |
| Nothing to act on | Useful: when to look up, where, and what you'll see. People send that to friends |

## 4. The formula every Reel follows

1. **0-1 seconds:** the bold claim in huge type over an image that's already moving. No logo intro, no "hey guys."
2. **1-10 seconds:** the payoff. A real image with a slow zoom, plus 2-4 short caption beats (when, where, how many, why it matters).
3. **Last beat:** the kicker and a send prompt ("Send this to who you're watching with"). The ending loops cleanly into the start.
4. **Length:** 8-15 seconds.
5. **Music:** an original ambient bed we generate ourselves. After Paycheck Math's jazz test, we don't touch anything that sounds like someone else's track.
6. **Cover:** the claim, readable as a grid tile.
7. **Caption:**
   - Line 1 repeats the hook.
   - 2-3 lines of facts.
   - A send or save prompt.
   - `Source:` and the image credit.
   - 3-5 hashtags.

## 6. Where the data comes from, and how it's checked

| Source | What we use | Cost | Cross-check |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Space Devs **Launch Library 2** | Upcoming launches, status, time, pad | Free (15 calls/hour) | RocketLaunch.live's free feed must agree on the date |
| **Spaceflight News API** | Headlines from ~40 space outlets (NASA, SpaceNews, NASASpaceflight, ESA…) | Free | Story discovery and links. Facts are confirmed at the primary source |
| **NASA Image & Video Library** | Photos and video | Free, public domain | Credit line from NASA's metadata |
| **ESA/Webb, ESA/Hubble** | New telescope images | Free, CC BY 4.0 | Credit required in the caption (automatic) |
| **NOAA Space Weather (SWPC)** | Aurora forecast (Kp index) and storm watches | Free | Kp forecast must match NOAA's official watch level |
| **JPL CNEOS** | Asteroid close approaches | Free | Distance math is rechecked; size comes from JPL's database |
| **US Naval Observatory**, **JPL Horizons** | Moon phases, distances, planet positions | Free | The two must agree within minutes or kilometers |
| **AMS + IMO** | Meteor shower peaks and rates | Free | Both must list the same peak night |
| **Wikipedia pageviews** | Trend radar: which space topics people are looking up right now | Free | Used only to pick topics, never shown as a fact |

**The rule from the other pages carries over:** every number gets a second source or an internal consistency check. Any failure **blocks the post**. Nothing wrong goes out.

## 7. Image rights (this protects future income)

- **OK:**
  - NASA, NOAA and USGS (public domain).
  - ESA/Webb and ESA/Hubble (CC BY 4.0, credited in the caption).
  - Our own graphics.
- **Never:**
  - SpaceX or Blue Origin photos and webcast clips.
  - TV and news-outlet footage, or other creators' clips.
  - Astronomy Picture of the Day images that carry a personal copyright.
  - The NASA logo, or anything that suggests NASA endorses us.
- **No AI-made images passed off as real.** Anything we draw is labeled "Illustration."
- **Photos are always described honestly.** A Perseid photo used for a Geminid post says "Photo: a 2021 Perseid meteor (NASA/Bill Ingalls)."

## 9. The feedback loop: how I decide what changes

**The numbers I watch, in order:**
1. **Sends per reach** (shares). This is the signal Instagram weighs most for showing posts to strangers.
2. **Non-follower reach.**
3. **Average watch time ÷ Reel length.**
4. **Follows per reach.**
5. **Saves.**

**Decision rules:**
- **Baseline:** ~100 reach is just the test audience.
  - A Reel that clears **500 reach** is a winner. It gets 2 spin-offs (same idea, new hook) within 48 hours.
  - A format that stays under half the median reach for 3+ posts gets **paused**.
- **One new experiment a week**, posted as Trial Reels (shown to non-followers first) where the API allows.
- **Planned tests, in order:**
  1. Narrated vs. text-only. A free open-source voice can run in GitHub's cloud.
  2. 8-second vs. 15-second Reels.
  3. Hook wording A/B (same visual, two hooks, a week apart).
  4. Midday vs. evening.
- Every change is written up in plain English in `tuning-log.md`: what changed, why, the numbers behind it, and what to watch.
