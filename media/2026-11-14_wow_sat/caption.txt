Every stage of star birth, in one shot 🤯

This is part of OMC-2, a cloud of cold gas and dust behind the Orion Nebula, 1,280 light-years from Earth. The scene is about 2.5 light-years across and holds every stage of star formation.
Jets from young protostars drive high-speed shockwaves into the gas around them; where they hit, the gas heats up and glows, making the sharp red ridges.
Thick gas and dust block all visible light from OMC-2. Only in infrared, which Webb sees, do the protostars shine through.
